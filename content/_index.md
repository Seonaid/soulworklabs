Each of us holds a perspective which is unique in the universe. We arrive in the world with a particular nervous system that leans towards or away from order, chaos, noise, certainty, possibility. We live through whatever childhood we have, and it reinforces or damps those inclinations... and yet. We have preferences. Certain things grab our attention.

These are what I refer to when I say, "Callings." These esoteric desires for something specific, regardless of its practical import or transactional value on the open market - **these are the universe noticing something about itself**. Through you.

But the doubts set in: "Is this the *most* important thing I could be doing? Isn't there something that would matter more? Shouldn't I be aiming for something more impressive?"

Yet we are not interchangeable. We might reason that something else matters more, but we still **find ourselves drawn again and again to an obsession that is hard to justify**. Drawing small birds. Throwing things long distances. The lifecycle of a particular species of flatworm.

The petulant part comes out: "Why can't my calling be cooler? Why can't it be easier and more lucrative? Why do I have to love this weird little thing so much?" *(Cue the cooing and snuggling of the weird little thing.)*

--- OR ---
<!--col2-->

At the other extreme, some visions feel so vast, so far beyond our capacity and our place in the world that even to consider them smacks of arrogance. We want to build things, to fix systems that are orders of magnitude larger than us. We (genuinely) want to lead cities, and states, and entire countries. Start movements. Become the best in the world at something.

Whether they are too big or too small, too early or too late, **we set our callings down for years at a time in pursuit of something more practical**. 

We earn money, raise families, solve problems, and become good representatives of whatever country and profession we belong to. We hide behind identities that are easy to explain, walk paths that are well-defined, and do work that "makes sense."

We may introduce ourselves with the job title we managed to wrest out of the hands of The System, but there's a part that still whispers, "That's not me. I know what I really am." It's the part that says, "Pay attention to this thing over here, this thing that seems pointless. This is what really matters."

Whatever it is that animates you - the local manifestation of the universe, the soul, atman, the self-aware pattern that has developed using your DNA and your experiences as its raw materials - cares about this thing. It resents the demands that pull it away, leaving the thing unnoticed, unremarked: The poem unwritten, the cat unpetted, the *huge project* unresourced.

I offer a provocation: What if resolving all these tensions is not our job? What if we simply need to wake up enough to do "The next right thing," and leave the unfolding to its own devices? What if moving through the world one conscious moment at a time were the whole point?
