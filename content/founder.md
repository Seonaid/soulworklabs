Soulwork Labs is a consolidation of work that has been spread across sites and conversations for the last thirty years. On one level it is a report on the application of reflective and embodiment practices to my own life. On another, it is a work of practical philosophy.

I have spent my life *obsessed* with moral reasoning, especially in technical professionals. I studied it in school. I wrote about it. I enticed people at technical conferences to talk about ethics over coffee. But my day-to-day never turned into a related role, even while it was obviously needed.

Instead I spent years on other things: caring for children and community, building things, using my skills of perception to document systems and provide guidance. Some of it was well-compensated and came with prestige. Much of it was unpaid and done on the invisible margins.

<!--rest-->

But always at the back of my mind there were questions: Why can't I leave this alone? Why can't I be content with the roles that have been assigned to me? Why do I insist that this work is Mine even when I can't point to any concrete outcomes after holding it for decades of my life?

> It may be that when we no longer know what to do, we have come to our real work — and when we no longer know which way to go, we have begun our real journey.
>
> — Wendell Berry



Each of us carries threads — inherited stories, patterns, obligations, held in the body. The practice is teasing them apart and holding each one up to the light: is this mine to keep weaving forward, or mine to cut? Most, examined honestly, get rewoven. Some were never inheritance so much as harm passed down disguised as inheritance — and the responsible thing is to stop them here, to metabolize what you were handed rather than pass it on.



I’ll be honest about the pull I still feel toward a more conventional, well-paid job with clear boundaries — one that’s easy to explain at a dinner party. That pull is real, and I’m not going to argue it away. Alongside it, just as real, is the sense that the pursuit of the Really Real is the correct use of a life — and that having done it long enough, it’s now right to offer it to others. The tension isn’t a war. It’s the honest cost of a calling, held with open eyes.

This is a lab, not a temple. Everything here is provisional and experimental by design — a feature, not a bug. I hold a fairly agnostic position on the nature of reality: closer to Spinoza’s God-or-Nature than to any system that claims to have it resolved. What I trust is the felt sense of alignment — moving *with* what is rather than against it — and I’m suspicious of anyone (including myself) who mistakes a tidy explanation for the thing itself.

If you’ve had real, uncanny, hard-to-explain experiences and stayed skeptical of the systems that rush to explain them — welcome. That don’t-know-mind is exactly the right instrument for this work.
