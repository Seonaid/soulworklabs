Soulwork Labs is a consolidation of work that has been spread across sites and conversations for the last thirty years. On one level it is a report on the application of reflective and embodiment practices to my own life (with instructions on how to replicate the bits that have worked over time). On another, it is a work of practical philosophy.

I have spent my life *obsessed* with moral reasoning, especially in technical professionals. I studied it in school. I wrote about it. I enticed people at technical conferences to talk about ethics over coffee. But my day-to-day never turned into a related role, even while it was obviously needed.

Instead I spent years on other things: caring for children and community, building things, using my skills of perception to document systems and provide guidance. Some of it was well-compensated and came with prestige. Much of it was unpaid and done on the invisible margins. And through all of it, I wrestled with questions much larger than offered by my immediate world, up to and including, "What does the universe want from me?"

> It may be that when we no longer know what to do, we have come to our real work — and when we no longer know which way to go, we have begun our real journey.
>
> — Wendell Berry

Each of us is midway through weaving a tapestry without a real pattern from the threads we have been handed. We add in lines that we have sought out or have simply tripped over, sometimes skillfully, but often awkwardly. There is no reason to believe that it will be coherent or that the stories we are navigating will play well with one another. We carry inherited concepts of obligation and coping mechanisms alongside traces of joy and what constitutes a life well lived. These stories live in the cells of our bodies: not just in our minds and our nervous systems, but at the level of the chemical cascades that we experience as emotions.

We are pulled and pushed by impulses and longings, yearnings and itches, by the need to maintain homeostasis and by the desire for growth. Some things we love. Involuntarily, irrationally, inadvisably. 

We each come to an idiosyncratic view of the world, no matter how "normal" we may try to appear.

Even after all these years, I wish I had been content with a conventional, well-paid job with clear boundaries... the type that’s easy to explain at dinner parties. I am "good at" a lot of things. But I *burn* for deep reality - and I'm here to invite you to join me on that quest.

I used to be an atheist but things got weird. Really weird. Really, really weird. Consistently weird. Yet I remain agnostic to the point of ambivalence. 

If you’ve also had real, uncanny, hard-to-explain experiences and stayed skeptical of the systems that rush to explain them — welcome. I have some things that might help.

This is a lab, not a temple. Everything here is provisional and experimental by design. It is also grounded in thousands of years of other people's teaching. This is not, "I tried this and it worked," but "I tried all of these things and I'm pretty sure that this one worked because of this other thing that is usually considered something else entirely."


What I trust is the felt sense of alignment — moving *with* what we have perceived rather than against it. I trust that we can tell the difference between the discomfort of the ego and the yearning that brings us closer to the living world and the consciousnesses that we share it with.
