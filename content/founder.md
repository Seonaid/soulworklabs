Soulwork Labs is a consolidation of work that has been spread across sites and conversations for the last thirty years. On one level it is a report on the application of reflective and embodiment practices to my own life. On another, it is a work of practical philosophy.

I have spent my life *obsessed* with moral reasoning, especially in technical professionals. I studied it in school. I wrote about it. I enticed people at technical conferences to talk about ethics over coffee. But my day-to-day never turned into a related role, even while it was obviously needed.

Instead I spent years on other things: caring for children and community, building things, using my skills of perception to document systems and provide guidance. Some of it was well-compensated and came with prestige. Much of it was unpaid and done on the invisible margins. And through all of it, I wrestled with questions much larger than offered by my immediate world, up to and including, "What does the universe want from me?"

<!--rest-->

Why can't I leave this alone? Why can't I be content with the roles that have been assigned to me? Why do I insist that this work is Mine even when I can't point to any concrete outcomes after holding it for decades of my life?

> It may be that when we no longer know what to do, we have come to our real work — and when we no longer know which way to go, we have begun our real journey.
>
> — Wendell Berry

Each of us is midway through weaving a tapestry from the narrative threads we have been handed. We add in lines that we have sought out or have simply tripped over, sometimes skillfully, but often awkwardly. We carry inherited stories of obligations and coping mechanisms alongside the traces of joy. They live in the cells of our bodies: not just in our minds and our nervous systems, but in the chemical cascades that we experience as emotions. 

Some things we love. Involuntarily, irrationally, inadvisably. We are pulled and pushed by impulses and longings, yearnings and itches, by the need to maintain homeostasis and by the desire for growth. And we each come to an idiosyncratic view of the world, no matter how "normal" we may try to become.

Even after all these years, I wish I had been content with a conventional, well-paid job with clear boundaries... the type that’s easy to explain at dinner parties. I am "good at" a lot of things. But I *burn* for deep reality.

This is a lab, not a temple. Everything here is provisional and experimental by design. 

I hold a fairly agnostic position on the nature of reality: closer to Spinoza’s God-or-Nature than to any system that claims to have it resolved. What I trust is the felt sense of alignment — moving *with* what is rather than against it — and I’m suspicious of anyone (including myself) who mistakes a tidy explanation for the thing itself.

If you’ve had real, uncanny, hard-to-explain experiences and stayed skeptical of the systems that rush to explain them — welcome. That don’t-know-mind is exactly the right instrument for this work.
