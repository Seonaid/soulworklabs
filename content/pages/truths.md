1. We each hold an idiosyncratic view of the world

No matter how "normal" we may try to appear, if nothing else, different roles demand different definitions of normal. But there is more to being human than fitting in, and more even than choosing which of definition to adopt from moment to moment.

The stories we have encountered about who we are and what we owe to each other live in the cells of our bodies: not just in our minds and our nervous systems, but at the level of the chemical cascades that we experience as emotions.

Underlying it all (all of it - the social machinations, the institutions, the technologies, the state-sponsored violence) there is a thread seeking homeostasis by looking to the future and securing resources, even at the expense of the health of The Whole.

A lot of the roles you have been assigned are at heart not about securing your future, but that of a larger group, which will sacrifice you if it deems you surplus.

The world has regularly thrown us new (old) insights into working with this challenge, which are variously useful. Some of them provide paths to liberation and some of them are designed to reinforce existing patterns of subservience. Most of them require skillful application and teasing apart one practice from another - when is it time to push harder and when is it time to accept? I frequently have considered titling all this work "The Wisdom to Know the Difference."

Each of us is midway through weaving a tapestry without a real pattern from the threads we have been handed. We add in lines that we have sought out or have simply tripped over, sometimes skillfully, but often awkwardly. There is no reason to believe that it will be coherent or that the stories we are navigating will play well with one another. We carry inherited concepts of obligation and coping mechanisms alongside traces of joy and what constitutes a life well lived. 

We are pulled and pushed by impulses and longings, yearnings and itches, by the need to maintain homeostasis and by the desire for growth. Some things we love. Involuntarily, irrationally, inadvisably.
